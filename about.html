About

I am a PhD student in Computing Science at the University of Alberta, working under the supervision of Dr. Irene Cheng . My research sits at the intersection of computer vision and clinical healthcare, with a focus on human pose estimation, motion analysis, and biomedical image processing.

My doctoral research focuses on developing and validating pose-based computer vision methods for quantitative gait and balance assessment from clinical video recordings. This work involves benchmarking state-of-the-art human pose estimation frameworks, including OpenPose, MediaPipe, MMPose and ViTPose, and evaluating their reliability against gold-standard gait assessment systems across diverse patient populations. My broader interests lie in translating computer vision and machine learning techniques into practical tools for clinical decision support and healthcare analytics.

Prior to my PhD, I completed an MSc in Computing Science (Multimedia) at the University of Alberta with a CGPA of 3.7/4.0, and a BTech in Computer Science and Engineering from Guru Nanak Dev University. I have also applied computer vision methods to agricultural and industrial domains, including automated orchard analysis and object detection/segmentation for industrial oil leakage inspection.
