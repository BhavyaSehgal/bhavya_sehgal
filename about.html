About

I am a PhD candidate in Computing Science at the University of Alberta, working under the supervision of Dr. Irene Cheng. My research sits at the intersection of computer vision and clinical healthcare, with a focus on human pose estimation, motion analysis, and biomedical image processing.

My doctoral work is supported through a Mitacs-funded collaboration with Okaki, where I develop and validate pose-based computer vision pipelines for quantitative gait and balance assessment from clinical video recordings. This involves benchmarking state-of-the-art pose estimation frameworks — including OpenPose, MediaPipe, and ViTPose — and evaluating their reliability against gold-standard GaitRite mat equipment across diverse patient populations.

Prior to my PhD, I completed an MSc in Computing Science (Multimedia) at the University of Alberta with a CGPA of 3.7/4.0, and a BTech in Computer Science and Engineering from Guru Nanak Dev University. I have also applied computer vision methods to agricultural and industrial domains, including automated orchard analysis and object detection for industrial inspection.
